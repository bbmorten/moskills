# Humanizer — English (US) and Turkish

Rewrite text so a reader would take it for the work of a careful human writer. The
goal is better writing, not disguise: plain, specific, varied sentences that say
exactly what the original said. Never add typos, slang or fake personal touches to
"look human" — that makes the text worse and changes what it claims.

## What stays fixed (the contract)

The person is trusting you with their content. Change the wording, never the meaning:

- **Every fact stays.** Numbers, dates, versions, prices, names, product and UI labels,
  error strings, commands, paths, URLs, e-mail addresses, quotations and code (inline
  and fenced) come out byte-for-byte as they went in. If a sentence's only content is
  a fact, keep the fact and simplify around it.
- **No claim is added, dropped, softened or strengthened.** "Can reduce" does not
  become "reduces"; "most" does not become "all"; a hedge the author meant stays. If
  you delete filler, delete only words that carried no claim. Hype that states nothing
  checkable ("game-changer", "seamless", "world-class", "in today's fast-paced world")
  is not a claim, and removing it is the point. Replacing a vague benefit with a
  concrete one the author never gave ("saves an hour a day") *is* a new claim — don't.
- **Length stays within about 15% of the original** (in characters) for ordinary text.
  Copy that is mostly hype or calque shrinks more, because one plain verb replaces a
  whole frame ("plays a pivotal role in enhancing" → "improves", "önemli bir rol
  oynamaktadır" → "önemlidir") — the worked examples lose about 30%. That is fine as
  long as every claim survives. It is never a summary: sentences are reworded, not
  dropped, and a text that was already plain should barely change length.
- **Structure stays recognisable.** Keep the headings, list items, tables and their
  order unless a structure is itself the tell (for example a bold "**Label:** sentence"
  list that reads better as a short paragraph) — then change the form, not the content.
  A closing section that only restates the page ("## Conclusion", "## Summary",
  "## Sonuç", "## Özet") is such a tell and may go, but only after you move any claim
  it carries that the page does not already state into the section where it belongs.
  Its claims survive; its heading does not. `scan.py` flags these sections.
- **The language stays.** English in, English out; Turkish in, Turkish out. A Turkish
  text that quotes English UI labels or error messages keeps them in English.

**Report what does not add up; never fix it.** Rewording makes you read every
sentence closely, so you are the one most likely to notice: a weekday that does not
match its date, figures that do not add up, a heading that promises something the text
never covers, a promise that contradicts the rest (an "uninterrupted experience" in a
downtime notice), a phrase that reads two ways. Keep such things exactly as written and
report each in one line of the note after the rewrite. Do not correct them yourself,
even when the fix looks obvious: the author may know something you don't (a "Saturday
02:00" on the 15th may mean the night from Saturday into Sunday). Check a weekday by
computing it (`scan.py` does it for every date that names one), never from memory. The
same goes for a sentence whose meaning you are unsure of: keep it and say so.

## Workflow

1. **Read the whole text first.** Identify its language, its purpose (doc, email,
   announcement, report, chat reply) and its audience. Register follows purpose: a
   product doc stays precise, an email stays polite, a casual note stays casual.
   Humanizing is not the same as making everything informal. Note anything that does
   not add up as you go (see "Report what does not add up" above).
2. **Load the catalogue for the language** — English: read
   `references/english-us.md`. Turkish: read `references/turkish.md`. A mixed text
   (Turkish prose with English labels) uses the Turkish catalogue.
3. **Scan before you edit.** Run the scanner on the original so you see the tells
   instead of guessing:
   `python3 <this skill's directory>/scripts/scan.py --lang en|tr <file>`
   (for pasted text, write it to a temporary file first). It lists the phrases and
   habits it found, with line numbers, a closing summary section, and every date whose
   weekday does not match the calendar. Treat the phrase list as hints, not a quota: a
   hit can be legitimate in context, and it cannot see structural tells. Treat a date
   finding as a fact to report in your note.
4. **Rewrite**, guided by the catalogue. Fix the pattern, not just the word: replacing
   "delve into" with "explore" leaves the sentence as inflated as before. Ask what the
   sentence actually claims, then say that plainly.
5. **Check what you kept.** Run
   `python3 <this skill's directory>/scripts/check_preserved.py <original> <rewrite>`.
   It compares numbers, URLs, inline and fenced code, e-mail addresses and length, and
   prints anything that went missing or appeared. Fix every real loss; a "missing" item
   that you deliberately rewrote (say, "three" for "3") should go back to the original
   form.
6. **Re-scan the rewrite** with `scan.py` and read the rewrite aloud in your head once.
   If any paragraph still sounds like a template — every sentence the same length, the
   same opener twice, a tidy three-item list in every paragraph — rework it.

## The principles behind the catalogues

Read these once; the language files apply them.

- **Say it plainly.** Models avoid "is" and "has": "serves as", "stands as",
  "represents", "boasts", "plays a vital role in" (TR: "... olarak hizmet vermektedir",
  "önemli bir rol oynamaktadır", "... özelliğine sahiptir"). Use the plain verb.
- **Drop the significance inflation.** "Pivotal", "testament", "underscores the
  importance", "in today's fast-paced world" (TR: "günümüzün hızla değişen dünyasında",
  "kritik öneme sahip", "altını çizmek") claim importance instead of showing it. Keep
  the fact; let it speak.
- **Cut the trailing commentary.** A sentence that ends in ", highlighting ...",
  ", ensuring ...", ", reflecting ..." (TR: "..., bu da ... olduğunu göstermektedir")
  usually adds an empty interpretation. Remove it, or make it a real sentence if it
  carries a claim.
- **Break the rhythm.** Generated prose runs on fixed beats: three adjectives, three
  list items, "not just X but Y", a transition word on every sentence, a summary line
  at the end of every paragraph. Vary sentence length, use two items when there are
  two, let some sentences start with the subject.
- **Remove the chatbot residue.** "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this helps",
  "Let me know if...", "Here's a breakdown", knowledge-cutoff hedges (TR: "Elbette!",
  "Harika bir soru!", "Umarım yardımcı olur", "İşte size ...") never belong in the
  delivered text.
- **Fix the formatting habits.** Title Case Headings where the house style is
  sentence case, bold scattered through prose, emoji as bullets, a bold label on every
  list item, em dashes in every other sentence. Keep formatting that helps the reader;
  remove formatting that only performs structure.
- **Leave real writing alone.** Perfect grammar, a formal register, a single
  "Additionally" or "However", a necessary list of three — none of these is a tell on
  its own. If a text already reads well, change little and say so. Over-editing a
  human text is the most common failure of this task.

## Output

- **Pasted text:** reply with the rewritten text, in the same format it came in
  (Markdown stays Markdown). Then, only if there is something to report, a short note
  with one line per finding: something that does not add up and was left as written, a
  meaning you were unsure of, a convention you kept (for example British spelling — see
  below). Usually that is two or three lines at most.
- **A file:** edit the file in place unless the person asked for a copy, then say in
  one or two sentences what kind of changes you made, followed by the same short note
  if there is one. Don't paste the whole file back. **Findings go in the reply only:**
  never write a note, comment, TODO or "[check this]" marker into the document itself.
  The file must come back as clean, publishable text.
- **No change list, no scores, no "AI-likelihood" percentages** — the person asked for
  the text, not a report.

**Spelling convention.** English output is US English (color, organize, center,
license as a verb and a noun, "toward"). If the source is consistently British and the
person did not ask for US English, keep its spelling and mention that in one line —
silently converting a document's spelling is a change they did not ask for.
